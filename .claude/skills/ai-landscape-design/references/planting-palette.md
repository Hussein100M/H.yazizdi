# Planting palettes and sizes

Image models render what they recognise. Use common English plant names with a mature height in metres — "Ghaf tree, 8 m, broad open canopy" lands; "drought-tolerant native species" produces generic blobs. Give the model the picture, not the spec sheet.

Pick the palette by climate, then take 2–3 species per layer. More than that and the scheme loses character; fewer and it looks like a parking lot.

## Arid Gulf and desert (Riyadh, Dubai, Doha, Kuwait)

- **Canopy trees:** Ghaf (Prosopis cineraria) 8–12 m, open filtered canopy; Sidr / Christ's thorn (Ziziphus spina-christi) 8–10 m, dense dark green; Neem 8–12 m; Acacia tortilis 6–8 m, flat umbrella head; Conocarpus 6–8 m for fast screening only.
- **Palms:** Date palm (Phoenix dactylifera) clear trunk 4–8 m; Washingtonia 8–15 m for tall vertical accents; Areca or Phoenix roebelenii 2–3 m in planters.
- **Shrubs:** Bougainvillea 1.5–3 m in mass or on a wall; Oleander (Nerium) 2–3 m for screening; Tecoma 1.5–2.5 m yellow flowering; Carissa 0.8–1.2 m as clipped hedge; Ixora 0.6–1 m.
- **Ground cover:** Lantana 0.3–0.5 m; Ruellia 0.3 m; Wedelia 0.2 m; Portulaca 0.15 m seasonal colour.
- **Lawn:** Paspalum or Bermuda, kept to one functional shape. Everywhere else use decomposed granite, buff gravel, or desert sand finish with boulder accents.
- **Character:** shade first. Buff and warm tones in the hardscape, deep green kept for where people actually gather, and the honest desert ground doing the rest.

## Coastal and Mediterranean

- **Canopy:** Olive 5–8 m silver-grey; Carob 8–10 m; Stone pine 10–15 m with a high umbrella head; Jacaranda 8–10 m; Poinciana / Flamboyant 8–10 m.
- **Palms:** Phoenix canariensis 6–10 m; Washingtonia for a formal line.
- **Shrubs:** Rosemary 0.8 m; Lavender 0.6 m; Pittosporum 1.5–2.5 m as hedge; Myrtle 1.5 m; Bougainvillea on walls and pergolas.
- **Ground cover:** Gazania 0.2 m; Rosmarinus prostratus 0.3 m; ornamental grasses 0.4–0.8 m.
- **Character:** silver-green foliage, gravel and stone, dry beauty, salt and wind tolerance at the sea edge.

## Temperate

- **Canopy:** Plane, Oak, Maple, Lime 10–20 m; ornamental Cherry or Magnolia 5–8 m for feature.
- **Shrubs:** Viburnum, Photinia, Box and Yew hedges 0.5–2 m.
- **Ground cover:** Vinca, Pachysandra, Hosta in shade, ornamental grasses.
- **Lawn:** generous and honest here — a temperate scheme can carry real lawn.
- **Character:** seasonal change, layered woodland edges, deeper greens.

## Tropical

- **Canopy:** Rain tree 12–18 m; Frangipani 4–6 m; Traveller's palm 6–8 m; mango and other fruiting canopy.
- **Palms:** Coconut 8–15 m; Areca clumps 3–5 m; Fishtail palm.
- **Shrubs:** Heliconia 1.5–2.5 m; Croton 1–1.5 m; Hibiscus 1.5–2 m; ginger 1–2 m.
- **Ground cover:** Philodendron, ferns, Alocasia, dense foliage mats.
- **Character:** layered depth, big coarse leaves, shade under a high canopy.

## Sizes and spacing to state in the prompt

| Element | Mature size | Spacing |
|---|---|---|
| Large canopy tree | 8–12 m high, 6–10 m spread | 8–10 m apart |
| Medium tree | 5–8 m | 6–7 m apart |
| Street tree line | 6–9 m | 7–9 m, even rhythm |
| Palm, single accent | 4–10 m clear trunk | grouped 3–5, 2–3 m apart |
| Palm, formal line | matched heights | 5–6 m apart |
| Large shrub / screen | 1.5–3 m | 1–1.5 m apart, continuous |
| Clipped hedge | 0.6–1.2 m trimmed | continuous, state the height |
| Low shrub mass | 0.6–1 m | 0.6 m, in drifts of 5+ |
| Ground cover | 0.15–0.4 m | continuous mat |
| Parking shade tree | 6–8 m | one every 4–6 bays |

## Rules that keep planting believable

- **Mass, don't dot.** Odd-numbered groups, drifts, and continuous beds. A single shrub alone in a lawn is a mistake in any climate.
- **Layer against the vertical.** Tall behind, mid in front, ground cover at the edge — so a bed reads as depth rather than a line of objects.
- **Vary within the species.** Slightly different heights and ages in the same group; perfectly identical trees are the clearest sign of machine planting.
- **Match the climate honestly.** A green English lawn in a Riyadh render destroys credibility faster than any geometry error.
- **Let the sun decide the shade.** Put canopy where people walk, sit, wait and park, on the side the sun actually comes from in the image.
- **Leave the architecture visible.** Trees frame the facade; they do not stand in front of it. In a hero view, keep the entrance axis open.
