# Project types — the brief behind the image

Read only the section that matches. Each one answers the same four questions: what the landscape is for, which zones the site naturally has, what the planting character should be, and what usually goes wrong.

## Private villa

- **For:** privacy, arrival, a shaded outdoor room the family actually uses.
- **Zones:** entrance forecourt and gate, driveway and car shade, boundary strip against the wall, side garden or majlis terrace, pool deck, back service yard.
- **Planting:** 2–4 feature trees only — a villa frontage drowns fast. Palms flanking the gate, a clipped hedge softening the boundary wall, massed shrubs at the wall base, a small lawn only if there is a real use for it.
- **Furniture:** none of the public kind. A shaded seating terrace, a pergola over the majlis, maybe a fire pit. No public benches, no bins, no bollard rows.
- **Goes wrong:** a forest in front of the facade; lawn wall to wall in a desert plot; a public-park bench on a private lawn.

## Residential compound

- **For:** shared life between the blocks — walking, children, shade, a reason to be outside the apartment.
- **Zones:** entry gate and guard, internal street with tree lines, block entrances, central green spine or courtyard, children's play, shaded seating pockets, parking screened by planting, service edge.
- **Planting:** street trees on a rhythm along the internal road (7–9 m apart), denser groups at the courtyard, a screening mid layer around parking, lawn only in the gathering courtyard.
- **Furniture:** benches at block entrances and along the spine, a shaded play area with soft surface, pergola over the seating court, bollard and path lighting.
- **Goes wrong:** identical trees at identical spacing across the whole scheme; a play area with no shade; seating with its back to the courtyard.

## Hotel

- **For:** the arrival sequence and the outdoor experience the guest pays for.
- **Zones:** drop-off and porte-cochère, arrival forecourt, feature planting on the entrance axis, pool deck and sun terrace, restaurant terrace, quiet garden, staff and service edge.
- **Planting:** formal and generous at arrival — matched pairs, clipped forms, a feature specimen on the axis. Looser and greener at the pool and garden. Fragrant, fine-textured planting where guests sit.
- **Furniture:** sun loungers in pairs with side tables, cabanas or parasols, lounge seating on the terrace, planters defining the restaurant edge, discreet lighting.
- **Goes wrong:** planting blocking the drop-off or the hotel signage; the pool deck turned into a garden so there is nowhere to walk; residential-scale plants at a monumental entrance.

## Resort

- **For:** an immersive landscape — the setting *is* the product.
- **Zones:** arrival, palm-lined spine, chalets or villas set in planting, pool and beach edge, boardwalk, shaded dining, spa garden.
- **Planting:** layered and lush within the guest areas, wind-tolerant and coarse toward the sea, palms for silhouette against the sky, planting used to give each unit privacy from the next.
- **Furniture:** loungers, day beds, hammocks, timber boardwalks, shade sails and palm-thatch parasols, low-level path lighting, outdoor showers.
- **Goes wrong:** urban street furniture in a resort; planting so heavy the water disappears; every unit landscaped identically.

## Public park

- **For:** the public — walking, sitting, playing, shade, events.
- **Zones:** entrances and gathering forecourt, main loop promenade, open lawn for events, shaded picnic groves, children's play by age, sport or fitness, quiet garden, water feature, kiosk and toilets, parking edge.
- **Planting:** large canopy trees carrying the shade of the promenade, groves rather than single trees, mixed shrub masses at edges, lawn where people actually gather, tough ground cover under trees.
- **Furniture:** benches every 25–50 m along the loop, shaded picnic tables in groves, pergolas or shade sails over seating clusters, drinking fountains, bins near seating and entries, path and bollard lighting, signage, play equipment on soft surface, fitness stations.
- **Goes wrong:** furniture scattered with no relationship to paths or shade; a promenade in full sun; play equipment with no seating for parents nearby; lawn everywhere in an arid climate.

## Plaza

- **For:** a hard urban space that holds people, events and movement, with green used surgically.
- **Zones:** open paved centre kept clear, tree groves or bosque at the edges, seating bands, water feature, café spill-out, framed entries.
- **Planting:** a formal grid or bosque of one tree species, raised planters with a single massed shrub, minimal lawn, feature trees at corners.
- **Furniture:** long linear benches and seat walls, movable café chairs, shade structures over the seating bands, in-ground uplights and mast lighting, bins and signage in a consistent family.
- **Goes wrong:** filling the open centre with planting and killing the plaza; fussy mixed planting where a disciplined grid was needed; seat walls with no shade.

## Campus

- **For:** movement between buildings, shade for waiting and studying, informal gathering.
- **Zones:** main entrance and forecourt, primary pedestrian spine, building entrance thresholds, courtyards between blocks, shaded study terraces, sports and open lawn, parking edge, service access.
- **Planting:** disciplined avenue trees along the spine, shaded groves at courtyards, hedges structuring the edges, tough ground cover in high-traffic zones, planting that survives crowds.
- **Furniture:** benches and study tables in the shade, pergolas over courtyards, bike racks near entrances, path and bollard lighting, wayfinding signage.
- **Goes wrong:** a decorative garden where people need to walk in numbers; shade in the wrong place; planting blocking the desire line so trampled shortcuts appear.

## Commercial

- **For:** visibility, comfortable arrival, and shaded outdoor retail or office space.
- **Zones:** frontage and signage line, entrance plaza, arrival drop-off, retail spill-out terraces, planted islands in surface parking, service screen.
- **Planting:** clean disciplined lines, raised planters with massed single-species shrubs, canopy trees in parking islands (one every 4–6 bays), nothing that hides the shopfronts or signage.
- **Furniture:** seating clusters outside the café frontage, planters as the edge between walking and sitting, shade over the terraces, catenary or pole lighting, bins integrated with the planter family.
- **Goes wrong:** planting across the shopfront or the sign; parking with no shade trees; over-fussy planting at an entrance that needs to read as one clean gesture.

## Civic

- **For:** dignity, arrival, and shaded waiting before a mosque, museum or public building.
- **Zones:** forecourt on the entrance axis, symmetrical flanking planting, ablution and service edge, shaded waiting, boundary planting.
- **Planting:** symmetry and restraint on the axis, matched pairs of feature trees, clipped hedges, palms where the local vocabulary expects them, a controlled palette rather than a mix.
- **Furniture:** simple seating at the edge of the forecourt, shade structures where people queue and wait, lighting that washes the ground rather than the building.
- **Goes wrong:** breaking the symmetry of a formal axis; planting that obstructs the ceremonial approach; a loose naturalistic scheme on a monumental building.

## Masterplan

- **For:** legibility from above — the green structure of the whole site.
- **Zones:** green spine, neighbourhood parks, street tree network, roundabouts and medians, buffers along main roads, wadi or drainage corridor, plot-level planting.
- **Planting:** read as pattern and texture from the air: continuous street tree lines that define the road hierarchy, larger canopy masses at parks, colour and bed shape used to mark centres.
- **Furniture:** not visible at this scale — indicate paths, parking, and shade structure footprints instead.
- **Goes wrong:** uniform green wash with no hierarchy; trees drawn at a scale that would make them 30 m wide; planting over road alignments and junctions.
