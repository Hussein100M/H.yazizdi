# Site furniture, hardscape and urban elements

Furniture is where AI landscape scenes fall apart: benches floating on lawn, a pergola over nothing, a path curving into a wall, lamps at doll-house scale. Give every element a **surface it sits on**, a **thing it faces**, a **dimension**, and a **count**.

Only bring in what the project type asks for — public furniture in a private villa is as wrong as a bare public park.

## Dimensions worth stating

| Element | Real size |
|---|---|
| Bench | 1.8 m long, seat 0.45 m high |
| Seat wall | 0.45 m high, 0.5 m deep, any length |
| Picnic table | 1.8 × 0.8 m, with two benches |
| Pergola | 3 × 4 m up to 4 × 8 m, clear height 2.6–3.0 m |
| Shade sail | 5–10 m span, 3–4 m at the low point |
| Parasol | 2.5–3 m diameter |
| Bollard light | 0.8–1.0 m high, 6–10 m apart |
| Pole light | 4–6 m high, 15–25 m apart |
| Pedestrian path | 1.8–3.0 m wide |
| Promenade | 4–6 m wide |
| Bin | 0.9 m high, near seating and entrances |
| Bike rack | 0.75 m high, near building entrances |
| Play equipment | 2–4 m high, on soft coloured safety surface |
| Planter | 0.6–1.2 m high, 1–3 m long |
| Water feature | state the footprint in metres and whether it is still or moving |

## Distribution rules

- **Seating.** Every 25–50 m along a promenade; clustered, not lined up, where people gather. Pairs facing each other at a stop, a row facing the view at an overlook. Back toward a hedge, planter or wall; face toward the space.
- **Shade.** Over seating clusters, play areas, waiting points and picnic tables — not as an isolated object in the middle of a lawn. In a hot climate, unshaded furniture is unused furniture, and it looks it.
- **Paths.** Connect two real destinations. Follow the direct line people would walk; curve only around something real — a tree group, a planting bed, a level change. Widen where paths meet, and add a small paved node with seating there.
- **Lighting.** Bollards along paths, uplights at feature trees and at the entrance, step lights at level changes. Keep the facade lighting as it is in the image unless the request is a night scene.
- **Play.** By age group, on soft surface, with shaded parent seating within sight, and a low fence or planting edge between play and any road.
- **Water.** One feature in one place, at the end of an axis or at the centre of a gathering zone, with seating facing it.
- **Bins, drinking fountains, signage.** Near entries, seating and junctions — sparse, in one consistent family.
- **Bike racks.** Near building entrances, on paving, not scattered on lawn.

## Paving and surfaces

Say what each surface is, because "path" without a material comes back as a grey smear:

- Main promenade: large-format grey or buff stone paving, or textured concrete.
- Secondary path: smaller unit pavers, or compacted decomposed granite in a natural setting.
- Gathering node: a contrasting paving pattern marking the place.
- Under trees in dry climates: gravel or decomposed granite, not lawn.
- Play: coloured rubber safety surface.
- Terraces and pool decks: timber or composite decking.
- Parking: keep the existing asphalt and bay markings — it is the first thing a model likes to redesign uninvited.

## The scale test before you write the prompt

Compare each element against your stated ruler. A bench is about a quarter of a car. A pergola is roughly one storey. A canopy tree in front of a two-storey villa reaches somewhere near the roof, not over it and not to the window sill. If a number does not survive that comparison, it will not survive the render.
