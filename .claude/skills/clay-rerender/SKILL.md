# Clay Re-render (Nano Banana)

Nano Banana is tuned to edit: it keeps the pixels it is given. Hand it a CG render or a model view with "keep everything" and it keeps the CG look too — flat light, plastic surfaces, glowing fixtures, pasted reflections. This skill splits the job into two Nano Banana passes and does every step by prompt. Exports and render passes from the 3D software are welcome but never required.

1. **Lit clay** — the same view as a white clay model, made by prompt from the material view (or an exported clay), lit the way the final photograph will be: the sun or daylight of the reference or the chosen time with its shadows, the sky and the air, the artificial lights in their lit or unlit state, and people added as white clay figures in natural places and poses. Everything that decides how the picture looks, except the materials, is set and checked here, on a simple white model, where light, shadows and people are far easier to judge and fix than on a finished photograph. Nano Banana keeps what it sees, so what passes the check carries into the photograph.
2. **Materials** — two images and one prompt. Image 1, the lit clay, sets the geometry, camera, framing, light, shadows, sky, air, fixtures and people. Image 2, the material view (the render, or the model view with textures or flat colours), sets which material and colour each surface takes. The words name each surface's real material and the reflections, and turn the clay figures into real people, so a CG or flat-coloured surface becomes a real material instead of a copy of its pixels, and the finished photograph is drawn once.

After that: local fix passes only for what drifted or what the review found (Prompt C), grain and vignette as Camera Raw sliders with no re-render, and Photoshop maps (Prompt D) only when the user asks.

Every pass re-renders the whole image, which softens fine detail a little and can drift, so compatible changes share a pass, the passes stay few, and each result is checked before the next.

- Talk to the user in Arabic (their dialect); prompts in English; notes in short Arabic.
- Hand over both prompts of the run (A and B) in ONE message up front, because the person runs them one after another in separate chats and gets lost when they arrive one at a time. The fix prompt (C) is held back: what it fixes is known only after a result comes back and is reviewed. The map prompts (D) are optional and written only when the user asks for them.
- Ask only through **AskUserQuestion** cards: max 4 questions, 2–4 options each, header ≤ 12 characters, recommended option first ending in "(مقترح)", never an "Other" option (it is built in). Skip any question the request or the images already answer and state that answer in one line.
- Headless run: ask nothing, take every recommended option, list them under الافتراضات.

## 1 Read the images (before any card)

Sort the attachments by look: the material view (a render, or a SketchUp or Revit view with textures or flat colours); a clay (an untextured white or grey copy of the same view), noting whether it already carries sun shadows; the realism reference (a photograph or render of another place); and, after a run, the result. The clay and the material view have to be the same view: a pair that doesn't line up in camera, framing or aspect gets blended into two views, so say so and ask for both from the same camera. With a clay and no material view, the description carries the materials alone: every material is ? unless the user named it, the read lists the elements, and the user fills in their materials by reply.

Then post one short block, max 8 lines, marking evidence ✓ seen · ≈ likely · ? cannot tell:

- **المبنى** — type (building or room); storeys as far as they show; openings per face; signature elements. Count only ✓.
- **المواد** — every surface in Image 2 by element and position, its look there and the real material it stands for (✓ cream walls → smooth cream render · ≈ speckled grey panels → light grey stone cladding · ? dark face left of the hall), ground, road and neighbouring buildings included. This becomes the material description; the user corrects it by reply.
- **تفاصيل باللون فقط** — details that exist only as colour on a flat surface in Image 2: flush insets, painted bands, lettering, a material change on one plane. Prompt A keeps them as fine outlines or shallow relief, and the description names them by position and count.
- **الكاميرا** — height, lens feel, verticals, aspect; whether the clay matches Image 2.
- **تفاصيل معرّضة** — the small things most likely to vanish or change: fixtures, boxes, grooves, fluted or ribbed panels, railings, kerbs and road lines, text and logos. They become the watch-list.
- **الإضاءة الصناعية** — every fixture by kind and position (sconces, coves, downlights, chandeliers, uplights, facade washers) and whether Image 2 shows it lit.
- **الأشخاص والنباتات** — count, kind and position of those already in Image 2 (they become real ones in the same places), and where added people would sit naturally without covering the signature elements.
- **المرجع** — its light as a photographer would read it: whether it has a readable sun, and if so its direction relative to the camera and its height; colour temperature, hard or soft, shadow pattern (dappled leaf shade, say), sky, haze, colour grade; what is physical in it (its building, furniture, plants, people), which stays out; overlays to crop first (app badges, icons, captions).

A ≈ or ? item is described by its look and never turned into a fact.

## 2 Card

One card, max 4 questions:
- **الكلاي** — only when no clay is attached: خلّ نانو يسوي الكلاي (مقترح) / عندي تصدير كلاي من البرنامج. An export is exact and skips the geometry check, so on that option wait for it before writing.
- **الواقعية** — only when no realism reference is attached: بأرفق صورة مرجعية (مقترح) / نهار بشمس جانبية / ذهبية / غيم بعد مطر; indoors: بأرفق صورة مرجعية (مقترح) / نهار ناعم / عصر ذهبي / مساء بالإضاءة الداخلية. On the first, wait for it; the others describe the light in words instead.
- **الأشخاص** — unless the request already says: outdoors شخصين يمشون (مقترح) / عائلة صغيرة / شخص واحد / بدون أشخاص; indoors شخصين في الجلسة (مقترح) / شخص واحد / عائلة صغيرة / بدون أشخاص. Each option's description says where they go and what they do in this view.
- **تأكيد** — when a main surface's material is ≈ or ?: the single most important one, 2–3 readings, the conservative one first with (مقترح). Every other unclear surface is described by its look and listed under الافتراضات.

The tool is not asked: the notes name Nano Banana Pro, or Nano Banana 2.1 with Thinking on high.

## 3 Decide (silently)

- **Sun on the clay** — whenever the reference has a readable sun, or a sunlit time is chosen, the clay takes that sun from the reference's direction relative to the camera and its height. It is asked for as direct, hard sunlight: the sunlit areas clearly brighter than the shade (about two stops), long dark shadows that are darkest and sharpest where each shadow begins and soften slightly with distance, and the shade kept dimmer — a light sentence that lifts the shade with ambient light gives weak shadows. The white clay takes the sun's colour temperature, so the warmth is set here too. With an overcast reference or no readable sun, the clay keeps soft even daylight with gentle occlusion. Leaf dapples only when the reference or the user wants them: cast from outside the frame onto the paving and the base of the walls, with the openings and the upper facade in clean light so the design stays readable.
- **Light** — from the reference, written as a photographer would set it: colour temperature, hard or soft, sky, haze, and the sun's direction. It goes into Prompt A after "lit like Image 2": the image shows the light, and the words keep the model on the light rather than on the reference's content. Direction is always given from the camera, with the side and whether the sun is behind the camera: "front-left" reads as a sun inside the picture, which backlights the facade and washes out the sky. Indoors, the sun comes through a window named by side and depth ("a tall window just outside the frame on the right, slightly ahead of the camera"), and the shadows are given by the way they fall ("to the left and slightly toward the camera"). Without a reference, the chosen time: day — sun from the left, behind the camera and outside the frame, about 30–35° high, raking across the facade, 5500K, clear blue sky with thin high cirrus; golden — the same side, about 15°, 3500K, long warm shadows; overcast after rain — soft even daylight, soft shadows only under projections, wet ground mirroring the building and the warm windows, layered grey cloud; indoors at evening — the daylight gone, the room lit by its own fixtures, the windows deep blue.
- **Sky and air** — in Prompt A, after the light: day or golden — "a clear warm late-afternoon sky with a few thin high cirrus, deeper blue at the top and pale and warm near the horizon"; overcast — "layered soft grey cloud, brighter toward the horizon"; outdoors, slight atmospheric haze makes the distant trees and buildings lighter and lower in contrast; indoors, the view through the windows takes the sky's place, a little brighter and hazier than the room, and a faint warm haze lets the sunlight show as soft beams. Never stacked twice.
- **Artificial lighting** — in Prompt A, every fixture by kind and position with its state for the time: daylight — unlit, or the coves at a faint glow; golden — a soft glow at most; dusk, night or indoor evening — lit, and the main light. A lit fixture is written by the light it throws on the surfaces around it, since a bare glow reads as CG: "the two wall sconces throw soft warm scallops of light up and down the wall", "the cove casts a soft warm wash along the ceiling edge", "the chandelier pools warm light on the table below", "the uplights graze the columns from the base". Colour temperature is named (2700–3000K for warm interiors), and lit fixtures stay dimmer than the sun when both are present.
- **People** — added in Prompt A as smooth white clay figures, as in an architectural model, so the light shades them and they cast shadows like everything else; Prompt B makes them real. Count and activity from the card; each one placed by position and action ("a woman seated at the left end of the sofa, reading"), sized against the doors, steps and furniture, in a calm everyday pose, turned across or away from the camera, never looking into it, never cut by the frame edge and never in front of the signature elements. Their dress is written in B: the user's words first; otherwise smart-casual and modest, suited to the climate, the season and the deliverable. People already in Image 2 become real ones in the same places.
- **Materials** — the description in Prompt B follows Image 2 surface by surface, by element and position, from the largest surfaces through the trims, openings and colour-only details to the ground, road and background, giving each its real material and colour ("the tall tower on the right is light grey stone cladding in flat panels with fine joints"). The user's words come first, then ✓; a ≈ surface is named by its look; a ? surface is described as it looks in Image 2 (colour and sheen). Every surface needs a real material, render and paint included ("smooth off-white mineral render with fine texture"), or a white or flat surface can stay chalky — on a clay base this matters most. Small accents in a contrasting colour (boxes, panels, planters) are named one by one, since the model tends to blend them into the main wall colour. Each colour-only detail is named with its exact position and count. Words suit natural materials better than colour codes, which push them toward flat paint; add a hex only for a painted surface whose exact colour matters. No invented stone names, sizes or elements the view lacks, because a listed element tends to appear.
- **Reflections** — in Prompt B, right after the materials, since they depend on them. Glass reflects what stands in front of it, so the reflections are written face by face from the view. From eye level, the upper panes reflect the sky of Image 1 and the lower panes the trees, forecourt and buildings at their height; a glass face that looks onto another part of the building reflects that part in its colour ("the side glazing of the tower reflects the blue-violet wall beside it"). Glass seen at a glancing angle reflects strongly, and glass seen head-on lets the interior show faintly through. Each pane reflects at a slightly different angle, so reflected lines step a little from pane to pane while the mullion grid stays crisp; one perfect mirror across a facade, black glass and flat blue glass are what give a render away. Glossy surfaces in the view are named once each: car paint and windows reflect the sky and the building's colours, polished stone holds a soft blurred reflection, satin metal a soft sheen of the sky; indoors, a polished floor reflects the furniture, the people and the sunlit patches as soft blurred reflections broken by its joints, strongest where it is seen at a glancing angle. At dusk or night the lit interiors fill the glass, with faint sky reflections on the upper panes.
- **Grade** — read from the reference and written in B in words ("muted warm greige, creamy highlights, softly lifted shadows, low saturation, gentle filmic contrast"), since the reference image goes into A only.
- **Plants** — the clay keeps trees and plants as white forms in their places; B makes them real ones of the kind seen in Image 2, same size and position. More planting, cars or styling belong to a later pass (Hand-off).
- **Deliverable** — "for a luxury real-estate brochure" for homes, "for an architecture magazine feature" for public and cultural buildings, or the user's own use; the model's planning step uses it to choose the finish.
- **Fixes** (after a result) — every drifted detail by element and position, written as its state in Image 2. Up to about four changes share a pass, the building before the site. An addition that improves the picture, such as a foreground frame of leaves, is the user's call before it is taken out.
- **Finish** — grain, vignette and highlight roll-off are Camera Raw sliders, with no re-render.
- Every default used goes under الافتراضات.

## 4 Write

Every prompt follows the same rules, because each one fixes a way edit prompts fail on Nano Banana:
- Flowing prose, no labels. A runs to about 220 words; B to about 150 plus the material description. The locks stay in one sentence: long lock lists are what make Nano Banana freeze the image.
- Name each image's role first. Without roles the model blends the images: it copies the CG look from the material view, and the reference hands over its buildings, furniture and plants along with its light.
- Put the change before the preservation and state the amount: "This is a full new photograph: every clay surface takes its real material, with real texture, joints, reflections and fine grain." Without the amount, the model treats the request as a light touch.
- Keep the preservation to one short sentence: geometry, openings, fixtures, camera, framing.
- Describe the wanted state in positive words. For the reference, that means saying where every object comes from instead of listing what to leave out of it: a list of exclusions names those things and invites them in.

**Prompt A — clay, light, atmosphere and people** (Image 1 the material view, Image 2 the reference):
```
Image 1 is a design render and the exact guide for the geometry, openings, camera and framing. Image 2 is a photograph that shows only the light, sky and atmosphere I want; every object in the result comes from Image 1. Turn Image 1 into a white clay model render of the exact same [building, site] and camera, lit like Image 2: every [wall, cornice, groove, panel, window, fixture, step and planter] keeps its exact shape, size and position, trees and plants become white clay forms in their places, a fine shallow line marks where two materials meet on one flat surface, lettering and insets stay as shallow relief in their exact shape, all surfaces are matte white clay and the glass is flat light grey. [The light sentence.] [The artificial lighting.] [The sky and the air.] [The people.] Keep the geometry, openings, fixtures, camera, framing and aspect of Image 1 exactly.
```
The light sentence, with a sun: "A single [warm late-afternoon] sun [from the left, behind the camera and outside the frame,] about [30°] high comes as direct hard sunlight: the sunlit surfaces are clearly brighter than the shade and carry the sun's warmth, and the real geometry casts crisp, dark shadows — under every cornice, slab and projection, from [the tower] onto the surfaces behind it, inside every reveal and down the steps — all in one direction, darkest and sharpest where each shadow begins and softening slightly with distance, with soft occlusion in the shade[, and soft dappled shadows from trees just outside the frame on the paving and the base of the walls]." Indoors: "A single low sun from [the right and slightly ahead of the camera], through a tall window just outside the frame on [the right], about [12°] high, comes as direct hard sunlight: broad patches of light lie across the floor [and the rug], much brighter than the rest of the room, and every object in them casts a long, dark shadow stretching [to the left and slightly toward the camera] — [the objects, by name] — darkest and sharpest where each meets the floor, while the rest of the room keeps a dimmer, soft light." Without a sun: "Soft even daylight with no direct sun adds gentle ambient occlusion in every groove, reveal and corner, with soft shadows only under projections."

The people: "[Two] people are added as smooth white clay figures, as in an architectural model: [each by position and action — a couple walking up the forecourt toward the steps, seen from three-quarter behind], in calm natural poses, sized against [the door], lit by the same light and casting their shadows in the same direction, clear of [the entrance]." Without people, the sentence drops.

Without a reference, drop the Image 2 sentence and "lit like Image 2"; the chosen time's light stands alone. Indoors it reads "the exact same room and camera", and the room's own elements are named (panels and mouldings, ceiling steps and coves, slats, curtains, furniture, fixtures). With an exported clay as Image 1, it opens "Image 1 is a white clay model and the exact guide for the geometry, openings, camera and framing", keeps the model white clay, and adds the light, the artificial lighting, the sky, the air and the people to it.

Filled example (a two-storey cream villa facade, a warm Mediterranean reference, two people):

Check the lit clay before going on: every opening, fixture, groove and colour-only detail from the watch-list present, and the camera, framing and aspect unchanged; every shadow in one direction and where the geometry puts it, dark and crisp where it begins, the sun itself out of the picture unless the reference shows it there; each fixture in its stated state, its light on the surfaces around it; the sky and the air as written; the people in count, place, scale and pose, clear of the signature elements, shaded and shadowed like the rest. Nothing physical from the reference. A clay whose geometry fails is regenerated, never used: what it lacks, B cannot bring back. A clay with the right geometry but weak light or wrong people gets a clay fix (Prompt C), so the geometry stays.

**Prompt C — fix** (written only after a check or a review, from what actually drifted; never handed over up front):

On the lit clay, when its geometry is right:
- The light: "Image 1 is a white clay model. Keep it white clay with the exact same geometry, people, camera and framing, and change only the light: [the light sentence, more precise — direction from the camera, height, 'direct hard sunlight', the objects that cast]."
- The people: "Using Image 1, keep everything exactly as it is and change only the people: [each by position, as wanted — 'the standing figure moves to the right of the steps, clear of the door' · 'the seated figure turns to face the window']."

On the photograph (Image 1 the result, Image 2 the material view):
```
Image 1 is the photograph and Image 2 is the original design view. Keep Image 1's light, sky, materials, textures, people, planting and colour grade, and change only these, each in its exact position: [up to about four fixes, each by element and position and written as its state in Image 2]. Everything else stays exactly as in Image 1.
```
How fixes read: "every element that is dark grey in Image 2 returns to matte dark grey: [the elements]" · "the entrance door is a solid matte black door, as in Image 2" · "the steps have a single black handrail on their right side, as in Image 2" · "the top-left corner is open sky where the branches were". Some fixes need no second image, and there the model's urge to preserve works for the design instead of against it:
- A wrong material: "Using Image 1, keep everything exactly as it is and change only [the surface, by position] to [its material]."
- The reflections: "Using Image 1, keep everything exactly as it is and change only the reflections: [the reflection clause]."
- A person who stayed statue-like or came out wrong: "Using Image 1, keep everything exactly as it is and change only [the person, by position]: a real [woman/man] [in …], with natural skin, hair, hands and fabric folds, in the same pose, place and size, lit by the same light." Hands and faces are fixed on a crop pasted back in Photoshop.
- A light touch: "Using Image 1, keep everything exactly as it is and change only the light: [the touch]" — "the dappled leaf shadows fall mainly on the paving and the lower wall, leaving the upper floor in clean sunlight" · "the shadow under the projecting upper floor is deeper and crisper". A light change bigger than a touch goes back to the clay (the light fix above), then B.

**Prompt D — maps** (optional, for Photoshop; written only when the user asks, and only the maps they ask for — all four when they just say "the maps"; each map in its own code block, run on the final photograph, which is Image 1 in every map prompt, and handed over with the Photoshop notes below in short Arabic). One prompt per map, each starting "Convert Image 1 into … of the exact same view, with the same camera, framing and aspect:"
- **Depth** — "a grayscale depth map …: brightness shows distance only — the nearest surfaces white, the farthest black, each surface one smooth gradient with no light, shadow or texture, crisp edges at every outline, the sky black."
- **Occlusion** — "an ambient occlusion pass …: no sun and no cast shadows; every surface white, with soft dark grey only in corners, grooves, reveals, under projections and where objects meet the ground."
- **Light only** — "a lighting pass …: every surface the same neutral matte mid-grey with no colour, keeping only the light, shadows and occlusion exactly as they fall in Image 1."
- **Reflections** — "a reflection pass …: only what the reflective surfaces reflect remains — the sky, trees and buildings in the glass, in the car paint and windows, in polished stone and metal — in its exact position, brightness and colour as in Image 1; the interior seen through the glass and every surface that does not reflect are pure black."
- **Normal** — not in the batch; through `normal-map-material-transfer` when asked.

"Distance only" and "no sun" matter: without them the depth map keeps the photograph's shading and the occlusion pass keeps its cast shadows, which then darken twice on Multiply.

In Photoshop a map works as a layer or as a mask (select it, copy, Alt+click the mask of an adjustment layer, paste). Depth, inverted, masks a haze layer and feeds Lens Blur; occlusion goes on Multiply at 15–30%; the light-only pass as the mask of a warming Color Balance layer warms only the sunlit areas, and inverted (Ctrl+I) on a cooling one cools only the shadows; the reflection pass goes on Screen at 20–50% to lift the reflections, and as the mask of a Curves or Hue/Saturation layer it grades only them. Nano Banana redraws, so each map goes over the photograph in Difference mode before use: edges that turn black mean it lands, and a shifted map is nudged with Free Transform or regenerated. Without the maps, Photoshop makes the same tools from the photograph itself: Filter → Neural Filters → Depth Blur → Output depth map only for depth, and Camera Raw's Color Grading for warm highlights and cool shadows.

## 5 Deliver

1. **Both prompts in one message**, in run order, each in its own code block with its label outside: برومبت أ — الكلاي والإضاءة والجو والأشخاص، برومبت ب — المواد. C is held back (it needs a clay or a result to look at), and D is not in the message: the closing line offers it. With an exported clay, A is written in its exported-clay form; B is written from the material view and doesn't wait for the clay, but runs only after the clay passes the check.
2. **ملاحظات** (Arabic, short):
   - الافتراضات.
   - ترتيب التشغيل (كل خطوة في محادثة جديدة، وانتظر فحص الخطوة قبل اللي بعدها)، مع الصور بالترتيب:
     1. أ: الرندر، ثم المرجع (لو فيه) ← كلاي أبيض فيه الشمس والظلال والسماء والجو والإضاءة الصناعية والأشخاص. افحصه، ولو الشكل صح بس الظلال أو الأشخاص ما ضبطوا أرسله لي وأكتب لك إصلاح على الكلاي نفسه.
     2. ب: الكلاي المضاء، ثم صورة المواد (الرندر) ← الصورة الواقعية. أرسلها للمراجعة.

## 6 Review

When a lit clay comes back, check it against the list under Prompt A and route it: geometry wrong → regenerate A; light or people wrong on the right geometry → the clay fix (C); reference objects in it → crop the reference and rerun A.

When a photograph comes back, compare it with the clay, the material view, the reference and the prompt, and give two scores:
- **مطابقة التصميم /10** — massing, storeys and roofline; openings by count and position; watch-list and colour-only details; each surface's material and colour against Image 2 and the description; fixtures in their stated state; people and plants in the same places and sizes as the clay; camera, framing and aspect; site lines (kerbs, roads, markings); nothing added, from the reference or anywhere else.
- **الواقعية /10** — the clay's light kept: warmth, shadow strength and direction, sky and air; nothing carried over from Image 2's CG look (flat light, plastic surfaces, fixtures lit by day); no surface left chalky or clay-like; lit fixtures read by the light they throw; people read as real (faces, hands, hair, fabric folds, contact shadows, scale), never statue-like; glass that reflects this photograph's own sky and surroundings, stronger at glancing angles and stepping slightly from pane to pane, never black, flat blue or one perfect mirror; ground and plants read as real.

Mark each check مطابق · فرق بسيط · فرق جوهري · غير قابل للتحقق; an unverifiable check never counts as a match, and any فرق جوهري in design means the result is not ready.

Fix the highest level first:
- Massing or camera drift → re-roll Prompt B; if the clay itself drifted, regenerate the clay first.
- Light, shadows or air wrong in the photograph while the clay had them right → re-roll B; when the clay had them wrong → the clay light fix, then B.
- Missing or changed details, wrong colours, an unwanted addition → write the photograph fix (C) now.
- A surface left chalky or copying Image 2's CG look → the one-line material fix, or rerun B with that surface described more concretely (colour, finish, joints). If the whole result copies Image 2, drop Image 2 and let the description carry the materials alone.
- A person still clay-like, or with wrong hands, face or scale → the one-image person fix, or a crop fix for hands and faces.
- Black, flat or mismatched reflections → the one-image reflection fix; a light that needs a touch → the light-touch fix.

A fix is done only once the regenerated image has been checked. After the fixes pass, the photograph is final; the maps come only if the user asks for them.
